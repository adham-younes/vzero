## Shared product patterns

1. **Make the assistant a staff-side case/document co-pilot.** Across sectors, the feasible common unit is: clarify the task, retrieve approved procedures with citations, identify missing inputs, draft a structured summary or message, and send it to a person for review.
2. **Use sector packs as evidence and templates, not as autonomous vertical products.** KnowledgeGrid can hold approved policy/procedure material; ServiceFlow can shape an internal intake-to-review flow. Keep each output grounded in customer-approved source material and label missing facts instead of filling gaps.
3. **Integration and governance determine the production boundary.** Current pasted text and small TXT/MD files support prototypes. Real customer cases require least-privilege access, retention controls, logging, secure connectors, role checks, and an explicit approval step. Public widget use should stay limited to approved public knowledge, never case-specific personal or operational data.

## Demand validation questions (no outreach performed)

Use synthetic cases, internal workflow observation by the product team, and opt-in research later. The evidence above establishes operational friction only; it does **not** establish willingness to pay, adoption, or ROI.

- Which role owns the specific task, and how often does it occur per week or month?
- What are the current steps, handoffs, systems, and time spent on one representative case? Where do omissions or rework happen?
- Which parts are safe to draft or check, and which decisions must remain with a licensed/authorized employee?
- Can a test use redacted/synthetic content and approved public or non-sensitive procedure text? What information must never enter the assistant?
- Would the team use a cited, reviewable draft in its existing workflow, or does the workflow require a CRM/helpdesk/EHR/OSS/BSS/claims/regulatory connector before any value appears?
- What observed behavior would count as value (fewer missing fields, less rework, faster handoff, shorter drafting time), and what baseline/measurement would verify it? Ask for a real historical example or workflow artifact rather than a hypothetical purchase promise.
- Who would approve a pilot, assess privacy/security, and own the output review? What procurement, localization and retention constraints apply in the target country?

**Planning implication:** Start architecture planning with banking complaint-document support and telecom provisioning/fault communication as the strongest reusable prototypes; test them on synthetic/redacted cases. Keep insurance and energy as follow-on workflow packs once integration and secure-data boundaries are designed. Treat healthcare administration as discovery-only until the scope can safely exclude PHI and clinical assertions. This ranking reflects fit with the described current product capabilities and source specificity, not market size or predicted revenue.
