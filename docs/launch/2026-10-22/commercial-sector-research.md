### Cross-sector patterns

1. **Exception work is more demoable than autonomous action.** In each sector, a useful small-assistant task can collect a messy input, surface gaps or repeated issues, and prepare a reviewable brief. The current app supports user-supplied text, retrieval/citations, chat, and an internal reviewed support proposal; it has no operational-system connectors.
2. **The missing information matters as much as the summary.** A defensible workflow should label unknowns, ask for missing context, and preserve source references. In logistics and construction especially, an assistant must not convert an unverified note into a factual cause, legal notice, or quantified impact.
3. **The evidence shows reported problems, not purchase intent.** These sources establish self-reported pressure or industry performance measures. They do not establish that SMBs will pay AGYNEXA, that this product resolves the root cause, or that a bilingual assistant changes outcomes. Validate with a consent-based pilot later, using opt-in participants, a real task sample, baseline task time/rework, output correction rate, and explicit purchase-intent questions; no outreach is part of this research.

### Demonstrations to consider first

1. **Retail returns review brief:** synthetic policy snippet plus anonymized, synthetic return-reason rows; show cited summary, ambiguity flags, and draft responses clearly labeled for manager review. The NRF source documents scale and tension between retailer/consumer priorities. This exercises text grounding and structured output with no integration claim.
2. **Distribution exception brief:** synthetic weekly table pasted as text with backorders, late shipments, inventory counts, and missing supplier paperwork; show clustered issues, unknowns, and owner questions. WERC confirms these are core professional metrics, while the demo should not imply a benchmark result or actual customer case.

Hospitality staffing with aggregate synthetic occupancy/staffing data is a suitable alternate demo. Pick the first demo based on which realistic sample data can be prepared cleanly, then validate the actual work problem with consent-based discovery. These are demonstrations of capability fit, not evidence of launch demand.

## Research gaps for the architecture planner

- **Logistics/foreign trade:** find a current, authoritative SME-focused source that quantifies paperwork/document completeness work separately from port/transit time, if this sector is to be prioritized.
- **Distribution:** the public WERC release supports metric relevance but does not reveal how many operators experience errors or the cost; avoid numeric pain claims without the full report or a better public source.
- **Willingness to pay:** not established in this research. Validate with opt-in task trials and direct buyer interviews only after the planning owner authorizes them.
