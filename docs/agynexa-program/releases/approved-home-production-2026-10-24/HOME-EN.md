# Build AI agents for your business operations

AGYNEXA is an Agent Builder for companies and enterprises. Use Studio to create agents, define their responsibilities and test their responses with the context your business provides.

Start with a role your team needs. Then map its place in the wider process: what it receives, what it should produce and when the work passes to another agent or a member of your team.

## Give each agent a business responsibility

Your team might need to prepare a case for review, assess a customer request or make sense of an operational exception. Each task has information it depends on, procedures it follows and someone who needs the result.

In AGYNEXA, you describe that work and the outcome you want. You configure the agent’s instructions and try them against situations your team encounters. A role can involve organizing supplied information, answering procedural questions, or preparing analysis and drafts for a responsible person to review.

When the process spans several specialties, the relationships between those roles become part of the system design.

## From a business process to an agent system

### 1. Define the starting point and the outcome

Identify what starts the process. It could be a customer request, a document awaiting review or a change in an operating condition. Describe what completion means and who needs the result.

This gives the agent a scope and makes clear which questions it must resolve before work moves on.

### 2. Assign responsibilities to distinct roles

A process may need one agent or several specialized roles. Map what each role handles, the information it needs and any results it depends on from another role.

Let the work determine that division. Checking whether a file is complete is a different responsibility from interpreting its contents or approving the resulting action.

### 3. Create the agents and provide their context

In Studio, create an agent, review its instructions and add the information its role needs. That context might include operating procedures, company policies or service details.

The instructions also establish how to handle missing information, when to ask for clarification and which decisions require a qualified reviewer.

### 4. Test each role against real situations

Use examples from your business, including incomplete requests and exceptions. Check whether the response addresses the task, what supports it and whether the next person can use the result.

Use those findings to refine the agent’s instructions and sources, and to identify work that still needs human judgment.

### 5. Design handoffs and system connections

In a multi-agent system, each handoff needs a definition: what starts the task, what reaches the next role and where the process pauses for review.

Running that design requires agent coordination and the integrations and permissions needed for actions in company systems. Configuring and testing agents in Studio is part of that journey. An action in an external application depends on a connection configured for that application and the access it permits.

### 6. Evaluate the work the system supports

Choose measures that reflect the process you want to improve: time spent preparing a case, completeness at handoff, rework and whether the responsible person can review the result.

These measures show where an agent’s role is useful and where the process design needs adjustment.

## How agent roles fit together

**An illustrative logistics system design**

When a shipment is delayed, an operations manager needs to understand the impact and available options before making a decision.

One agent could review the case and identify missing details. Another could assess options against operating rules, while a further role prepares a customer update. Each role uses defined results from the preceding work, and the operations manager retains the consequential decision.

The coordination design establishes task order, information handoffs and how to handle conflicting or incomplete information. Making actual updates requires connections to the relevant operating systems and communication channels.

## Design around the work in your industry

The responsibilities change with the business. Start with the operational problem, available information and decision the team needs to make.

| Industry | Starting problem | Intended outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Logistics | Delay and exception details are scattered across several parties. | A coherent case that helps an operations manager assess options and coordinate a response. |
| Energy and industrial operations | Maintenance and operating notes arrive incomplete between teams or shifts. | Clearer handover of information and outstanding work, with technical decisions retained by qualified staff. |
| Distribution | Details get lost between a customer inquiry, order preparation and delivery exceptions. | A complete case that helps teams continue the work and identify decisions that need an owner. |
| Professional services | Client requirements change without a clear account of the effect on scope and delivery. | A structured assessment of the change for the project owner to review. |

## Give agents the company context their work requires

Role instructions establish an agent’s responsibility. Company knowledge supplies the context for carrying it out. An approved procedure explains how to handle a case, service information helps interpret a request, and details supplied by the user establish what applies to the task.

Studio supports answers grounded in the information you add, with references you can inspect. When that information is insufficient, the missing material becomes part of the review before the result is used.

## Keep human decisions in the workflow

Your team may need to review a recommendation, resolve an exception or approve a commitment to a customer. Place these decisions within the process and identify what the responsible person must receive to make them.

For any action connected to an external system, define the required access and how success will be confirmed. Preparing a proposal, approving it and carrying out the action remain distinct steps.

## Build the first role in your agent system

Choose a process your team knows and a responsibility that needs support. Create an agent in Studio, provide the business context and test it on cases whose results you can review.

Use what you learn to refine that role and establish how it fits with the rest of the system.
